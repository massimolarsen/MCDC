\noindent Every RMC kernel write-up in this directory evaluates the \emph{lab-frame}
density $f_L(\Eout, \mulab \mid \Ein)$ of one emitted neutron per unit outgoing energy
and lab scattering cosine (azimuth integrated), times the reaction yield. This note
collects the transformations they share. Code: \texttt{mcdc/rmc/kinematics.py},
\texttt{mcdc/rmc/reaction.py}.

\section{Center-of-mass to lab, inelastic and fission}

For inelastic and fission spectra whose data frame is COM, MC/DC draws
$(\Ecm,\mucm)$ there and converts by classical velocity addition, with $A$ the target
atomic-weight ratio:
\begin{equation}
  \Eout = \Ecm + \frac{\Ein + 2\mucm (A+1)\sqrt{\Ein\Ecm}}{(A+1)^2},\qquad
  \mulab = \mucm\sqrt{\frac{\Ecm}{\Eout}} + \frac{1}{A+1}\sqrt{\frac{\Ein}{\Eout}} .
\end{equation}
The inverse used by the evaluators is
\begin{equation}
  \Ecm = \Eout + \frac{\Ein}{(A+1)^2} - \frac{2\mulab\sqrt{\Ein\Eout}}{A+1},\qquad
  \mucm = \frac{\mulab\sqrt{\Eout} - \sqrt{\Ein}/(A+1)}{\sqrt{\Ecm}},
\end{equation}
and a lab point with $\Ecm<0$ is unreachable. The density formulas use
$\Ecm>0$; zero-speed degeneracies are interpreted by their distributional limits.
The frame flag is part of the source data (\endfspec{MF=6, LCT});
lab-frame spectra do not undergo this COM transformation.

\paragraph{Joint Jacobian.} The lab velocity is $\bm v = \bm V_c + \bm w$ with $\bm V_c$
the (fixed) COM velocity, so the velocity density is translation invariant:
$p_v(\bm v) = p_w(\bm w)$. With $E \propto v^2$, $\dd^3 v \propto \sqrt{E}\dd E \dd\mu
\dd\phi$ in both frames, and the azimuth is uniform in both, so
$p_v = f_L / (2\pi c\sqrt{\Eout})$ and $p_w = f_C / (2\pi c\sqrt{\Ecm})$ give
\begin{equation}
  f_L(\Eout, \mulab \mid \Ein) = f_C(\Ecm, \mucm \mid \Ein)\,\sqrt{\Eout/\Ecm}.
\end{equation}

\section{Two-body (line) kernels}

Under classical kinematics, for a fixed COM energy $\Ecm$
(classical elastic or discrete levels: ACE Law 3),
$\Eout$ is affine in $\mucm$,
$\left|\dd\mucm/\dd\Eout\right| = (A+1)/(2\sqrt{\Ein\Ecm})$, and each $\Eout$ fixes one
$\mulab^*(\Eout)$. The lab density is a line,
\begin{equation}
  f_L = g(\Eout \mid \Ein)\,\delta\big(\mulab - \mulab^*(\Eout)\big),\qquad
  g = Y\,p_c\big(\mucm^*(\Eout)\big)\left|\frac{\dd\mucm}{\dd\Eout}\right| ,
\end{equation}
with $p_c$ the COM angular density and $Y$ the yield. Elastic scattering in MC/DC adds
velocities with the relativistic speed--energy relation; its energy map and Jacobian
are distinct from the classical ones above, as derived in \texttt{elastic.tex}.

\section{Azimuthal integration about the slab axis}

For an incident polar cosine $\mu$ (about $z$) and lab scattering cosine $\mulab$, the
outgoing polar cosine is $\mu' = \mu\mulab + \sqrt{1-\mu^2}\sqrt{1-\mulab^2}\cos\phi$
with $\phi$ uniform, whose density is the arcsine kernel
\begin{equation}
  K(\mu, \mu', \mulab) = \frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{1 - \mu^2 - \mu'^2 - \mulab^2 + 2\mu\mu'\mulab}},
\end{equation}
symmetric in $(\mu, \mu')$. Its integral over a cosine bin $[\mu_a, \mu_b]$ is analytic:
\begin{equation}
  \Pi(\mu', \mulab; \mu_a, \mu_b) = \frac{1}{\pi}\Big[\arcsin t\Big]_{t_a}^{t_b},\qquad
  t = \operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}\frac{\mu - \mu'\mulab}{\sqrt{1-\mu'^2}\sqrt{1-\mulab^2}}.
\end{equation}
These density and arcsine expressions apply on the allowed support with a positive
radicand and nonzero denominator. The density is zero outside the support.
When $\sqrt{1-\mu'^2}\sqrt{1-\mulab^2}=0$, the distribution is a delta function
at $\mu=\mu'\mulab$, and bin membership gives its integral.
RMC uses the incident-bin integrated kernel
\begin{equation}
  \bar\tau_j(\Ein \to \Eout, \mu') = \int\dd\mulab\, f_L(\Eout, \mulab \mid \Ein)\,
  \Pi(\mu', \mulab; \mu_{j-1/2}, \mu_{j+1/2}),
\end{equation}
in closed form ($g\,\Pi(\mu', \mulab^*)$) for lines and by adaptive Gauss--Kronrod over
$\mulab$ (split at the kinks of $\Pi$) for continuous laws. These pieces are verified
by the unit tests in \texttt{test/unit/rmc/test\_kinematics.py}; the lab densities
$f_L$ of the supported laws are compared against MC/DC's samplers in the individual
write-ups. The shared verification note states the statistical and quadrature
limitations of those comparisons.
